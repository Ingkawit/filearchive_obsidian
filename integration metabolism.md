
- upstream molecule may positive feedforward downstream
- downstream molecule may negative feedback upstream
- regulating factors:
	- redox state (NADH/NAD+ ratio)

#### hormones:
- [[insulin]] - anabolic (building up) [[fed state]]
	- leads to dephosphorylation (activating building-up enzymes)
		- more glycolysis
		- more synthesis of glycogen, lipid, protein
		- less breakdown of glycogen, lipid, protein
		- less liver gluconeogenesis
- [[glucagon]] - catabolic (breaking down) [[fast state]]
	- leads to phosphorylation (activating breaking-down enzymes)
	- *muscle have no glucagon receptor*
		- less glycolysis
		- less synthesis of glycogen, lipid, protein
		- more breakdown of glycogen, lipid, protein
		- more liver gluconeogenesis
- [[epinephrine]] - catabolic
	-  leads to phosphorylation (activating breaking-down enzymes)
		- less glycolysis in liver/ more glycolysis in heart
		- less synthesis of glycogen, lipid, protein
		- more breakdown of glycogen, lipid, protein
		- more liver gluconeogenesis

#### cellular compartmentation
- **mitochondria** matrix
	- acetyl coA synthesis
	- [[Krebs Cycle]], [[electron transport chain]]
	- [[beta-oxidation]] of fatty acid
	- [[ketogenesis]] (@liver)
	- [[urea cycle]] (first 2 steps; CPS-I, OTC)
- **cytosol**
	- [[glycolysis]], [[pentose phosphate pathway]]
	- [[fatty acid synthesis]], [[cholesterol synthesis]] (both NADPH-driven)
	- glycogen synthesis [[glycogenesis]] and [[glycogenolysis]]
	- [[urea cycle]] (last 3 steps)
	- [[gluconeogenesis]]
	- [[pyrimidine synthesis]] (CPS-II)
- **smooth endoplasmic reticulum**
	- lipid/steroid synthesis
	- triglyceride package for lipoproteins
	- cytochrome P450 system
	- **glucose-6-phosphatase** (@liver,kidney)
- **peroxisome**
	- [[beta-oxidation]] of VLCFA
	- catalase for H2O2 detox
- **lysosome**
	- sphingolipid, glycolipid degradation


- ketogenesis
	- beta-oxidation triggers ketogenesis
		- because beta-oxidation creates high acetyl coA and high NADH at the same time
	- glycolysis doesn't trigger ketogenesis

**isozyme and organ specialization**

liver can store glycogen in larger ratio
- glucokinase in liver less inhibited by glucose-6-phosphate
- hexokinase in muscle better inhibited  by glucose-6-phosphate
- glycogen synthase in liver less inhibited than in muscle

muscle
- lactate dehydrogenase has lower Km for pyruvate (favor pyruvate -> lactate)
- PFK-2 always active -> high F2,6BP -> stimulate PFK-1 -> glycolysis

heart
- sufficient oxygen supply -> aerobic metabolism
- lactate dehydrogenase has lower Km for lactate (favor lactate -> pyruvate)

epinephrine rises 
- PFK-2 in liver inhibited, PFK-2 in heart activated
- PFK-1 in liver low activity, PFK-1 in heart high activity
- liver glycolysis slow down, heart glycolysis increased

adipose tissue has high Km for triacylglycerol, high insulin can activate LPL
cardiac muscle LPL increased when low insulin, prefer fatty acid when fasting

adipose tissue doesn't have glycerokinase (glycerol -> glycerol-3-phosphate)
adipose tissue synthesize  glycerol-3-phosphate from DHAP instead

brain
- GLUT1 has low Km for glucose
- low beta-oxidation -> low acetyl coA and citrate -> PFK-1 active -> glycolysis
- can catabolize BCAAs
- prolonged starvation, use ketone bodies to save glucose for RBC

kidney
- renal cortex has gluconeogenesis activity
- can use glutamine, glutamine -> glutamate releasing NH3 for pH regulation

liver
- anabolic enzymes have low Km
- have glucose-6-phosphatase
- lack BCAA transaminase
