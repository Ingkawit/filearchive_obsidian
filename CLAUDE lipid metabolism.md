## Lipid Metabolism — Full Revision

_Compiled and connected from your Obsidian vault. Structure: lipid classes → fatty acid handling (synthesis/oxidation) → ketone bodies → cholesterol → lipoprotein transport → bile → eicosanoids → clinical correlates._

---

## 1. Lipid — the big picture

**[[lipid]]** building blocks: glycerol + **[[fatty acid]]**

Types:
- Fatty acid and derivatives
  - **[[glycerolipid]]** — triacylglycerol, glycoglycerolipid
  - **[[phospholipid]]**
    - glycerophospholipid: phosphatidylcholine, PE, PS, PI, PG, cardiolipin
    - ether glycerolipid: plasmalogen, platelet activating factor
  - **[[sphingolipid]]** — ceramide, sphingophospholipid
- Isoprene-derived
  - steroid: **[[bile salt]]**, sex hormones
  - sterol: fat-soluble vitamins (A D E K), **[[cholesterol]]**
- **[[eicosanoid]]**

Complexity tiers: simple (TAG, wax) → complex (phospholipid, glycolipid, lipoprotein) → precursors/derivatives (fatty acid, glycerol, cholesterol)

---

## 2. Fatty acids — structure and nomenclature

**[[fatty acid]]**: long hydrocarbon chain + terminal carboxylic group
- **Saturated** — no double bond → high melting point → solid at room temp (palmitic, stearic)
- **Unsaturated** — double bond(s) → low melting point → liquid at room temp
  - MUFA (mono-), PUFA (poly-)

Carbon numbering:
- **α** = 2nd carbon (1st excluding COOH)
- **β** = 3rd carbon (2nd excluding COOH) — site of β-oxidation cleavage
- **ω** = last carbon — defines omega-3/omega-6 family

---

## 3. Triacylglycerol (TAG) — storage and mobilization

**[[triacylglycerol]]**: glycerol + 3 fatty acids. Functions: energy storage (adipose), essential FA delivery, organ protection, thermal insulation.

**Storage**
- **Adipocyte**: [[lipoprotein lipase]] hydrolyzes TAG in chylomicron/VLDL → free FA taken up → re-esterified to TAG; stimulated by **insulin** in the [[fed state]]
- **Hepatocyte**: glycerol taken up to synthesize TAG in fed state

**Precursors**
- glucose → [[DHAP]] → [[G3P]] (glycerol backbone)
- fatty acyl-coA (acyl chains)

**Lipolysis** (adipocyte)
- controlled by **hormone-sensitive lipase**
- releases free FA into blood bound to albumin

This is the fasting-state counterpart to fed-state storage — see [[fed state]].

---

## 4. Fatty acid synthesis (anabolic — cytosol)

**[[fatty acid synthesis]]**
- Site: cytosol of hepatocyte, adipocyte
- **Rate-limiting step**: acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC): acetyl-CoA → malonyl-CoA
  - coenzyme: **biotin (B7)**
- Final product: **palmitate (C16:0)**

**Elongation** (at ER)
- uses malonyl-CoA to add 2-carbon units
- **Desaturation**: mammals cannot insert a double bond beyond C9
  - Essential fatty acids must come from diet
  - linoleic acid (ω-6) → arachidonic acid → eicosanoids
  - α-linolenic acid (ω-3) → EPA → anti-inflammatory eicosanoids (see [[omega-3]])

This directly feeds into **[[arachidonic acid]]** and the **[[eicosanoid]]** pathway below (section 9).

---

## 5. Fatty acid oxidation (catabolic — mitochondria/peroxisome)

**[[fatty acid oxidation]]**
- Site: mitochondria (may pre-shorten in peroxisome for VLCFA)
- No FA catabolism in RBC (no mitochondria) or brain (FA can't cross blood-brain barrier)
- Products: acetyl-CoA, NADH, FADH2; costs 2 ATP to activate FA (add CoA)

| size | carbons | site of catabolism | membrane transport | activation of FA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SCFA | 2–6 | mitochondria | diffusion | cytosol or mitochondria |
| MCFA | 7–12 | mitochondria | diffusion | mitochondrial matrix |
| LCFA | 13–22 | mitochondria | carnitine shuttle (CPTI/CPTII) | outer mitochondrial membrane |
| VLCFA | >22 | peroxisome → mitochondria | peroxisomal transport | peroxisome |

Defective peroxisomal VLCFA handling → **[[Zellweger syndrome]]** accumulation of VLCFA.

Two oxidation types: **[[beta-oxidation]]** (main pathway) and **[[omega-oxidation]]** (backup pathway).

### 5a. Beta-oxidation
**[[beta-oxidation]]**
- Rate-limiting step: **carnitine acyltransferase I (CATI)/CPTI**; completed by translocase + CPTII at inner membrane
- Mechanism: oxidize the β-carbon, cleave between α and β carbons

**Even-chain FA**
- Per cycle: 1 acetyl-CoA, 1 NADH, 1 FADH2
- Key enzymes: acyl-CoA dehydrogenase (→FADH2), β-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase (→NADH)
- Rounds of β-oxidation = (carbons/2) − 1

**Odd-chain FA**
- Final round yields acetyl-CoA + propionyl-CoA → succinyl-CoA → OAA → glucose (via [[gluconeogenesis]])
- Key enzyme: propionyl-CoA carboxylase, coenzyme **biotin (B7)**
- Clinical: biotin deficiency → high propionyl-CoA → acidemia/aciduria

**Clinical (carnitine shuttle defects)**
- Primary carnitine deficiency (can't transport carnitine in): low FA use → hypoketotic hypoglycemia, muscle damage → Tx: oral carnitine, high-carb/low-fat, avoid fasting
- Secondary (CPT1/CPT2 defect): FA can't enter mitochondria
  - CPT1 deficiency → hypoglycemia
  - CPT2 deficiency → accumulation of long-chain acylcarnitine → cardiac arrest
  - Tx: high-carb diet, MCFA supplement (bypasses carnitine shuttle), avoid fasting

### 5b. Omega-oxidation
**[[omega-oxidation]]**
- Site: ER
- Produces water-soluble dicarboxylic acid, excreted in plasma/urine
- Clinical: MCAD (MCFAcyl-CoA dehydrogenase) deficiency → low β-oxidation → hypoketotic hypoglycemia; excess MCFA shunted to ω-oxidation → dicarboxylic aciduria. Tx: high-carb diet, avoid MCFA (found in milk)

## 6. Ketone bodies — the fasting-state fuel

**[[ketone body]]** types: acetone, acetoacetate, D-β-hydroxybutyrate

### 6a. Ketogenesis
**[[ketogenesis]]**
- Site: liver mitochondria
- Rate-limiting step: **HMG-CoA synthase**
- Trigger: fasting state (low glucose, high glucagon) → lipolysis → FA → β-oxidation → acetyl-CoA → ketone bodies
- Transport: dissolves directly in blood plasma (no lipoprotein needed)

### 6b. Ketolysis
**[[ketolysis]]**
- Site: mitochondria of muscle, brain, kidney, heart
- **Liver cannot use ketone bodies** (lacks SCOT enzyme)
- Mechanism: succinyl-CoA (from Krebs) donates CoA to acetoacetate → acetoacetyl-CoA → acetyl-CoA → Krebs cycle
- Prolonged fasting: brain switches to ketone bodies, sparing glucose (reduces gluconeogenesis demand)

**Clinical**: Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA, type 1 DM) — ketoacidosis (low blood pH), fruity breath (acetone), ketonemia, ketonuria

This links back to section 5 (β-oxidation supplies the acetyl-CoA) and to whole-body fuel switching alongside [[gluconeogenesis]].

## 7. Cholesterol metabolism

**[[cholesterol]]**
- Structure: 4 fused hydrocarbon rings + hydroxyl group
- Properties: non-caloric, nonsaponifiable, precursor of steroid hormone, bile acid, vitamin D; maintains membrane fluidity

**Liver cholesterol pool**
- Input: diet → chylomicron; extrahepatic synthesis → HDL; hepatic synthesis
- Output: VLDL secretion; free cholesterol in bile; conversion to bile acid/bile salt
- Excretion: in feces after bile secretion

**Disease**
- Smith-Lemli-Opitz syndrome — cholesterol deficiency
- Familial LCAT deficiency — decreased HDL → dyslipidemia → cardiovascular disease
- Excessive ACAT/cholesterol → cardiovascular disease
- Bile salt deficiency → decreased cholesterol solubility → fat malabsorption (steatorrhea) → fat-soluble vitamin deficiency → cholesterol saturation → gallstone

### Cholesterol synthesis pathway (cytosol)
acetyl-CoA → acetoacetyl-CoA → HMG-CoA → (**HMG-CoA reductase**, rate-limiting; target of **[[statin]]**) → mevalonate → isoprene → squalene → cholesterol

- Excess free cholesterol → esterified by **ACAT** → cholesteryl ester (storage form)
- HDL reverse transport: **LCAT** esterifies cholesterol picked up from peripheral tissue
- Downstream fates: bile salt, steroid hormone

## 8. Lipoproteins — how lipids travel in blood

**[[lipoprotein]]** transports lipids in plasma. Composition gradient (chylomicron → HDL): TAG decreases, protein increases.

| lipoprotein | function | major protein |
|---|---|---|
| chylomicron | dietary fat transport | apoB-48 |
| VLDL | liver fat export | apoB-100 |
| IDL | VLDL remnant | apoB-100 |
| LDL | "bad cholesterol" | apoB-100 |
| HDL | "good cholesterol" | apoA-I, apoA-II |

**Apolipoproteins**
| apo | associated lipoprotein | function |
|---|---|---|
| apoA-I | HDL-2 | activates LCAT, anti-atherogenic |
| apoB-48 | chylomicron | structural |
| apoB-100 | VLDL, IDL, LDL | ligand for LDL receptor |
| apoC-II | chylomicron, VLDL | activates lipoprotein lipase (LPL) |
| apoE | chylomicron, VLDL, IDL, LDL | ligand for hepatic uptake via LDL receptor |

### Exogenous pathway (diet)
small intestine → chylomicron (apoB-48) → lymphatic system → blood capillary → LPL (activated by apoC-II) strips TAG at muscle/adipocyte → chylomicron remnant → liver uptake (apoE)

### Endogenous pathway (liver export)
liver → VLDL (apoB-100, apoE, apoC-II) → LPL strips TAG at peripheral tissue → VLDL remnant → IDL → hepatic triacylglyceride lipase further strips TAG → **LDL** → LDL receptor-mediated uptake (apoB-100) at liver/peripheral tissue

### HDL / reverse cholesterol transport
nascent HDL (apoA-I) picks up free cholesterol from peripheral tissue → **LCAT** esterifies it (cholesterol ester) → **CETP** exchanges cholesterol ester for TAG with VLDL/LDL → mature HDL delivers cholesterol back to liver

**Atherogenesis link**: excess LDL → oxidized LDL (oxLDL) → taken up by macrophages → foam cell → atherosclerotic plaque. This connects to [[familial hypercholesterolemia]] below.

### Disorders of lipoprotein metabolism
**[[hyperlipoproteinemia]]**

| type | too much lipid | too much lipoprotein | disease | treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | TG, CHO | chylomicron | LPL defect, apoC-II defect | avoid TG & LCFA, provide MCFA |
| IIa | CHO | LDL | familial, LDL receptor defect, apoB-100 | statin, bile acid sequestrant |
| IIb | CHO, TG | LDL, VLDL | familial, apoB defect | — |
| III | CHO, TG | IDL | apoE defect | — |
| IV | TG, CHO | — | familial, LPL defect, multifactorial | — |
| V | TG, CHO | VLDL, chylomicron | familial, apoA defect, multifactorial | — |

**[[hypolipoproteinemia]]** (abetalipoproteinemia): low CHO/TG (no VLDL, no chylomicron), caused by MTP protein mutation.

**[[familial hypercholesterolemia]]**: LDL receptor deficiency → high LDL in blood → LDL oxidation → foam cell formation → xanthoma (eye fat deposits). Treatment: bile acid sequestrant.

## 9. Bile — cholesterol's excretory and digestive fate

**[[bile]]**: produced in **[[liver]]**, stored in **gallbladder**. Composition: bile acid, bile salt, free cholesterol, bilirubin (waste pigment).

**[[bile acid]]**: formed in liver ER from cholesterol via **cholesterol 7-α-hydroxylase (CYP7A1)** — the rate-limiting step of bile acid synthesis.

**[[bile salt]]**: bile acid conjugated with an amino acid; amphipathic — emulsifies dietary fat for digestion/absorption (feeds into the exogenous chylomicron pathway, section 8).

This closes the loop: cholesterol (section 7) → bile acid/salt (section 9) → emulsifies dietary TAG → chylomicron formation (section 8) → LPL releases FA → β-oxidation or storage (sections 3, 5).

## 10. Eicosanoids — signaling lipids from arachidonic acid

**[[arachidonic acid]]**
- Formed by elongation + desaturation of linoleic acid (an essential FA, see section 4)
- Stored in membrane phospholipid (phosphatidylcholine)
- Released by **phospholipase A2**

**[[eicosanoid]]**
- Autocrine/paracrine mediators; very unstable, synthesized on demand
- Regulate inflammation and immune response

| product | source | function |
|---|---|---|
| prostaglandins (PGE, PGF) | endothelial cell | vasodilation / vasoconstriction |
| prostacyclin | endothelial cell | inhibits platelet aggregation |
| thromboxane | platelet | promotes platelet aggregation |
| leukotriene | leukocyte | bronchoconstriction, inflammation |

**[[eicosanoid synthesis]]** — occurs in ER, two branches from arachidonic acid:
- **Cyclooxygenase (COX) pathway** (cyclic) → prostaglandins, thromboxanes
  - COX-1: housekeeping — gastric mucus maintenance, renal blood flow
  - COX-2: inflammatory enzyme
- **Lipoxygenase (LOX) pathway** (linear) → leukotrienes

**[[omega-3]]**: fish oil ω-3 PUFAs (EPA, DHA) shift eicosanoid metabolism away from ω-6 → increase PGI3 (anti-platelet) and TXA3 (weak platelet aggregation) → lower LDL/VLDL → less platelet aggregation, less atherosclerotic plaque, lower MI/stroke risk. Directly ties back to section 8 (atherogenesis).

## 11. Master summary — how it all connects

1. **Dietary/stored fat in** → TAG (§3) is broken down by LPL (§8) into free FA + glycerol.
2. **Fasted state**: FA undergo β-/ω-oxidation (§5) in mitochondria → acetyl-CoA. Excess acetyl-CoA (liver, high glucagon) is diverted to **ketogenesis** (§6a) since acetyl-CoA cannot re-enter gluconeogenesis directly. Other tissues run **ketolysis** (§6b) for fuel, sparing glucose.
3. **Fed state**: insulin promotes TAG re-esterification and storage (§3) and drives **fatty acid synthesis** (§4) from excess acetyl-CoA/citrate in the cytosol.
4. **Essential fatty acids** (linoleic, α-linolenic — can't be made past C9, §4) become **arachidonic acid** and feed the **eicosanoid** signaling system (§10), which governs inflammation, platelet aggregation, and vascular tone — modifiable by dietary ω-3 intake.
5. **Cholesterol** (§7) is synthesized from acetyl-CoA (same starting material as FA synthesis) via HMG-CoA reductase (statin target) and is packaged into **lipoproteins** (§8) for transport, ultimately either used for steroidogenesis, membrane building, or excreted as **bile acid/salt** (§9), which in turn is required to emulsify and absorb dietary fat — closing the loop back to §3/§8.
6. Defects anywhere in this network produce the clinical syndromes scattered through the notes: carnitine shuttle defects (§5), biotin deficiency (§4, §5), Zellweger syndrome (§5), MCAD deficiency (§5b), DKA (§6b), LDL receptor/apoB/apoE/apoC-II defects → hyperlipoproteinemias (§8), abetalipoproteinemia (§8), familial hypercholesterolemia (§8).
